Four things happen while you’re busy

No buttons, no shortcuts to remember. It runs in the background and only acts when a call genuinely goes unanswered.

1

It sees the missed call

The phone rang and went quiet without being picked up. Answered calls, rejections and outgoing calls are left alone.

2

Your chance to call back first. If you do, the reply is cancelled — nothing is sent after the conversation already happened.

First call, second call, third — each can say something different. Add time windows or limit it to unknown numbers.

WhatsApp through the server bridge, or SMS straight from the phone. Nothing appears on screen, and the device can stay locked.

Questions

Is there an iPhone version?

No, and there can’t be. iOS does not let an app see missed calls in the background, send a text on its own, or drive WhatsApp. Android only.

Why isn’t it on Google Play?

Play restricts the SMS permission to apps that are your default messaging app. Installing the APK directly avoids that, and keeps the app free of any account or tracking.

Do I need the invite key?

Only for WhatsApp. Text replies work the moment you install it. The key links your phone to the bridge that sends WhatsApp messages on your behalf.

Will it reply after I’ve already called back?

No. Just before sending, it re-reads the call log. If you called them back, or answered a later call from the same number, the reply is cancelled and the log records why.

Does WhatsApp show anything on my screen?

Not in the default mode — the bridge sends it from the server while your phone stays locked. There is an optional on-device mode that opens WhatsApp and taps send, but it needs the screen unlocked and is off by default.

I have a Xiaomi, Redmi or POCO phone.

Then the setup wizard has extra steps for you. MIUI kills background apps unless Autostart is on and the battery policy is set to “No restrictions”. The app walks you to both, and warns you on the home screen if the system starts blocking it again.

Which file do I download?

arm64-v8a covers essentially every phone sold in the last several years. If you’re unsure, take Universal — it’s larger but runs anywhere.
